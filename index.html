Photokitchen Schedule Desk

Methodology — how it thinks, and why it's worth using

The problem this replaces

Every new project means opening Town Hall, scanning for existing shoots, meetings, and leaves, then counting lead days by memory or an old Slack thread to land on a timeline. Add a second or third project running at the same time, and that whole check has to be repeated for every combination, by hand, every time something shifts.

What it actually does

The scheduler reads the real timing patterns from 2026 Photokitchen history — the lead times PK, accounts, and Plate This have actually used — and generates two ready-to-review timelines from a project's Signed CE (or Shotlist) date. It checks each one live against the current Town Hall board and against any other project you're scheduling at the same time. It never books anything on its own — it proposes, flags, and waits for you.

The core logic, in five rules

1.The Signed CE / Shotlist date never moves on its own. Everything else in the chain is calculated from it, using either that account's historical lead times or your own edited ones.
2.Weekends are never proposed. A date landing on Saturday or Sunday is nudged to the nearest weekday automatically — never shown as a suggestion.
3.Holidays and confirmed shoots are hard blocks. The generator will not suggest a date that lands on a holiday or an already-confirmed shoot — those are skipped before you ever see them.
4.Everything else is a flag, not a block. A tentative Town Hall shoot, a teammate's leave day, or two projects landing on the same shoot day still get proposed — but clearly labelled Soft Conflict (worth a second look) or Real Conflict (needs a decision) so you decide, not the tool.
5.Multiple projects watch each other. Add a second or third project and the scheduler checks their pinned timelines against each other the same way it checks Town Hall — no manual cross-referencing.

What stays entirely yours

Every date can be dragged, typed over, or edited by hand. Every field's lead time is yours to change. Nothing is ever sent to Slack or written to Town Hall without you choosing to copy it and paste it yourself. The scheduler's job is to do the counting and the cross-checking — the judgment calls are still entirely KC and Chin's.
